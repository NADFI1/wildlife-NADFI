Protecting Nature, Life and Building Hopes.

The newly identified wild cat species is the tilcayo (Leopardus tilcayo), announced in September 2026 as the first new living cat species formally described in over a century. [1] Key Facts About the Tilcayo Location: Native to Bolivia’s mountainous, cloud-covered Yungas forest ecoregion. [1, 2] Size: Extremely tiny, measuring about 18 inches long and weighing roughly 3 pounds (1.4 kg)—making it smaller than a typical house cat. [1, 2] Appearance: Features a light brown coat patterned with dark, irregularly shaped leopard-like rosettes. [1, 2] Classification: Belongs to the South American tiger cat group, which genetic studies recently expanded into a complex of distinct species. [1, 2] Conservation Status: Research was led by National Geographic Explorer Paola Nogales-Ascarrunz. The only known living individual currently resides in protective care at the Senda Verde Wildlife Sanctuary in Bolivia after being rescued from a home where it was briefly kept as a pet. [1, 2, 3]
